When a tank car vents or a line lets go, the questions are immediate — where does it go, who's under it, do we shelter or evacuate. Too often the answer is stuck behind desktop software in a command vehicle, a phone call to a reachback modeler, or a tool that never gets the result onto the team's map.

    Every model runs the same way the plume you just watched does — computed live, validated against the reference science, ready for the call you actually get.

    CHEM

    Gaussian plume

    Pasquill-Gifford dispersion with Briggs (1973) sigmas, AEGL/ERPG thresholds, 48 chemicals.

    CHEM

    Dense gas

    Heavier-than-air correction for chlorine, phosgene, and vapor clouds that hug the ground.

    EXPLOSIVE

    Blast overpressure

    Brode (1955) with Hopkinson-Cranz scaling — structural-damage and injury rings.

    EXPLOSIVE

    BLEVE fireball

    RAD

    Radiological (RDD)

    Cloudshine dose-rate contours with EPA FGR-12 dose conversion factors.

    FIRE

    Fire / smoke

    Heskestad flame height into a buoyant PM2.5 & CO plume for structure and wildland fires.

    MOVING

    Line source

    Superimposed release along a route — a leaking rail car or tanker on the move.
